How marketers use AI in Europe: three findings

I looked at marketing work in the Anthropic Economic Index across all 33 European markets. The picture is different from Asia-Pacific, and the differences say a lot about where AI earns its place in a marketing team.

01Email, not content, is the biggest marketing job

In Asia-Pacific, content creation dominates. In Europe the largest marketing-relevant output is email and messaging, averaging about 4.5% of Claude usage across the 33 markets. Planning and strategy comes next at about 4.0%, with written marketing content at about 2.5%. Presentations, creative writing and images trail behind.

Content's share also slipped between April and May 2026 in 20 of the 33 markets, while it was growing across most of APAC.

What I'd do with it: start with the inbox, not the blog. Replies, outreach and stakeholder updates are where European teams already lean on AI, so that's where a shared prompt library or brand voice guide pays off fastest.

02The North plans, the East and South produce

Compare how much each market uses Claude to make marketing content against how much it uses it to plan it, and Europe splits in two:

  • Execution-led: Ukraine produces about 1.25 times as much content as it plans, and Malta is level. Serbia, Cyprus and Portugal follow.
  • Strategy-led: Iceland plans about 3.7 times more than it produces, with Luxembourg, Norway, Ireland and Sweden close behind.

The same split shows up in how much content gets made at all. Ukraine, Cyprus and Malta have the highest content shares, around 3.8 to 3.9% of usage. Iceland and Norway have the lowest, around 1.2 to 1.3%.

What I'd do with it: if you run a European team from a Nordic or Western hub, expect AI to show up in planning documents first. If your production sits in Eastern or Southern Europe, it's probably already in the content itself, so put your review process there.

03The heaviest adopters mostly plan

Relative to working-age population, Europe's top adopters are Switzerland (about 5.0 times the expected rate), Luxembourg (4.9), Norway (4.0) and Iceland (4.0). Three of those four, Luxembourg, Norway and Iceland, are also among the most strategy-led markets on the continent, with some of the lowest content shares. Ukraine sits at the other end: below-average adoption, but the highest content share in Europe.

It isn't a rule. Malta adopts almost as heavily as Norway and still produces as much as it plans. But it echoes Asia-Pacific, where Australia leads adoption and the Philippines leads marketing output: the heaviest users tend to use AI to think, not just to produce.

What I'd do with it: don't measure AI maturity by output volume. A team producing less content with AI may be further along, using it for the decisions that shape what gets made.

About the data

Source: the Anthropic Economic Index, May 2026 release, published under CC-BY. It covers Claude.ai chat and Cowork usage on Free, Pro and Max plans, and excludes enterprise API usage and other AI tools.

Output types are inferred from what each conversation is about, and shares are a percentage of each market's total Claude usage. Averages here are simple averages across the 33 markets. The planning-to-production comparison divides each market's content share by its strategy share. The adoption index compares a market's share of Claude usage with its share of working-age population. The figures describe how Claude is used, not how many people work in marketing or how jobs are changing.
