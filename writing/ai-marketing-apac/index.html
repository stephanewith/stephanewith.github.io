How marketers use AI in Asia-Pacific: three findings

I took the Anthropic Economic Index and looked only at marketing work across 18 Asia-Pacific markets. Three patterns stood out, and each one changes how I'd plan an AI rollout for a marketing team.

01Content creation is the default use, almost everywhere

Content creation and copywriting is the single largest use of Claude in 15 of the 18 published APAC markets, and a top-two use in all 18. Its share runs from about 20% of usage in Cambodia to 32% in Indonesia, against 22.7% globally. It also grew in 14 of the 18 markets between April and May 2026.

The exceptions are telling: software development leads in Cambodia, and education leads in Nepal and Mongolia.

What I'd do with it: if your team's first AI use case is "write more content", you're not ahead, you're at the baseline. The advantage moves to what happens around the copy: briefs, testing, distribution and measurement.

02Search roles automate, creative roles collaborate

Split the conversations by the marketing role they match, and a clean gradient appears between automation (handing a whole task to the AI) and augmentation (iterating with it back and forth):

  • Automation-leaning: Search Marketing Strategists delegate about 55% of tasks, Market Research Analysts about 46%.
  • Balanced: Marketing Managers and PR Specialists, around 38 to 39%.
  • Augmentation-leaning: Advertising and Promotions Managers (about 33%), Graphic Designers (about 30%), and Writers and Authors (about 29%).

The more structured and measurable the work, the more of it gets handed over. The more it depends on taste and brand judgement, the more people keep themselves in the loop.

What I'd do with it: don't run one AI playbook for the whole marketing team. Give search and analytics roles agents and scheduled workflows. Give creative roles better collaboration: brand context, examples and fast iteration.

03The highest adopters aren't the biggest content producers

Adoption is highest in the established markets. Relative to its share of working-age population, Australia uses Claude at 6.4 times the expected rate, followed by Singapore (5.8), New Zealand (4.8) and South Korea (3.8). Usage there is heavily work-focused.

But the most marketing output comes from elsewhere. Emerging markets such as the Philippines and Pakistan have lower overall adoption, yet the highest share of marketing content. In the Philippines, marketing and social assets make up 4.4% of usage, against a 2.6% global benchmark.

What I'd do with it: if you work with offshore or regional content teams, assume they're already producing with AI at volume. The conversation to have is about brand standards and review, not whether to start.

About the data

Source: the Anthropic Economic Index, April to May 2026 release, published under CC-BY. It covers Claude.ai chat and Cowork usage on Free, Pro and Max plans, and excludes enterprise API usage and other AI tools.

Roles are matched from what each conversation is about, not from verified job titles, so "Search Marketing Strategists" means conversations matched to that role's tasks. The adoption index compares a market's share of Claude usage with its share of working-age population. The figures describe how Claude is used, not how many people work in marketing or how jobs are changing.
